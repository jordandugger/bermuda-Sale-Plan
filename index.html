2. FSBO vs agent

  • Post-NAR settlement (Aug 2024): buyer-agent pay cannot be advertised on FMLS or GAMLS. Buyers sign brokerage agreements before touring. Sellers may still pay the buyer side, negotiated in the offer as a concession.
  • Georgia average total commission about 5.74% (2.89% list, 2.85% buyer). On $1.8M every 1% is $18K; the listing-side saving is about $52K.
  • NAR 2025: FSBO was 5% of sales, 88% of buyers used an agent, 40% of FSBO sellers did not actively market. Nothing suggests FSBO outperforms in luxury lakefront.
  • Middle options: Redfin, Clever partner agents, 1% listing brokers, flat-fee full-service brokers ($3K to $7K). A 1% to 1.5% Lanier specialist may earn it by fielding dock-permit questions. Interview two or three; none were verified by name.

3. What a Georgia FSBO sale looks like

Closing attorney (mandatory)

  • Georgia Supreme Court (UPL Advisory Opinion 2003-2): only a licensed GA attorney may close a real estate transaction or prepare the deed. No separate escrow or title company; title insurance is issued through the attorney's agency.
  • The attorney runs title, prepares the deed, collects the Chase payoff, files the PT-61 transfer tax return, disburses, records.
  • GAR F201 gives the buyer the right to select the closing attorney; in a financed deal the attorney represents the lender. Buyer usually pays the closing fee; seller pays deed prep. Retain our own seller's counsel now, about $500 to $1,500.

Forms

  • GAR F201 Purchase and Sale Agreement (6-1-26 revision) is what every buyer agent will submit. GAR forms are member-licensed; flat-fee services like beycome include them, and any GA attorney can supply them. No generic forms.
  • GAR F301 Seller's Property Disclosure. Georgia is caveat emptor but known latent defects must be disclosed even as-is (Wilhite v. Mays). Disclose: upstairs moisture (cause, fix, invoices, any mold testing), Corps file (D00742 history, 9/17/24 cancellation, reinstatement, deficiency notice, dock correction, riprap status), septic, pool, insurance claims, STR history, any unpermitted dock mods.

Contract flow

  1. Offer on F201 with exhibits: F301, legal description, financing exhibit, appraisal contingency, furnishings bill of sale.
  2. Binding Agreement Date: every deadline counts from it.
  3. Due diligence 10 to 14 days: buyer can walk for any reason and keep earnest money. Expect inspection, septic, pool, dock, Corps permit verification, STR income review.
  4. Earnest money 1% to 2%, held by the closing attorney (FSBO) or buyer's broker.
  5. Financing and appraisal: about half of buyers are cash. Furnishings and STR income will not appraise.
  6. Amendment to address concerns, then close.

Stipulations to expect or propose

  • Seller delivers active USACE Shoreline Use Permit, approved site plan, and written no-open-violations confirmation within 5 days of Binding Agreement Date.
  • Buyer files USACE change-of-ownership promptly after closing.
  • Furnishings convey as-is for $0 separate consideration. Handling of future Airbnb reservations.

What Claude can and cannot do

  • Can: summarize and compare offers, deadline calendar, plain-English F201 walkthrough, draft counters and stipulations for attorney review, draft F301 answers, listing copy and data room, emails to agents, Corps and contractors, net math.
  • Cannot: act as attorney, close, prepare or supervise the deed, hold earnest money, guarantee enforceability. Those are the practice of law in Georgia. Claude drafts, attorney approves, we sign.

How the permit transfers at sale

  • USACE permits are nontransferable. The buyer applies for change of ownership; processing about 6 to 8 weeks once complete. Package: recorded deed, plat and site layout, dock drawings, Exhibit C if the dock has power. Local agents cite 14 to 30 days after closing to submit. Fee not published ($485 in our 2023 records vs $835 cited by one brokerage).
  • Lanier buyer agents routinely write permit-verification stipulations. A buyer cannot be guaranteed a permit for a non-compliant dock. Selling as-is with disclosure is legal but will cost more than finishing the work.

Upstairs moisture

  • Likely causes: upstairs balcony/deck flashing and door thresholds, roof-to-wall or skylight flashing, attic air-handler condensate line or pan, bath exhaust venting into the attic, summer condensation on cold ducts or a short-cycling AC.
  • Sequence: independent moisture and mold assessor (not a remediator) with infrared and meter mapping, sampling if stained. Fix the source. Remediate if needed. Post-remediation clearance letter. Keep every invoice for the F301 packet.
  • Then a pre-listing inspection of the whole house (baseline June 30, 2023 Platinum Plus). Add septic pump-and-inspect, pool check, HVAC service.

Data room to build in January

  • 24 months Airbnb payouts, expenses, occupancy; Hall County STR license; tax and insurance bills; Corps file and dock drawings; inspections and repair invoices; furnishings inventory.
